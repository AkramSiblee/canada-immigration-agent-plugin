# Losing refugee protection: cessation, vacation, danger opinions

IRPA current to 2026-10-29 (last amended 2026-03-26). No 2026 amendments shown on ss.108, 109, 115; s.40.1 and s.46 unchanged since 2012/2017 (H).

## Must cover
- s.108 cessation grounds, s.109 vacation, consequences for PRs, s.115(2) danger opinions, PRRA limits, travel advice

## Cessation (s.108) (H)
- Minister (CBSA/IRCC) applies to the **RPD** (s.108(2)). Grounds (s.108(1)):
  - (a) **voluntarily re-availed** of the protection of the country of nationality (classic trigger: getting/renewing and **travelling on the home-country passport**, trips back)
  - (b) voluntarily **reacquired** nationality
  - (c) acquired a **new nationality** and its protection
  - (d) voluntarily **re-established** in the country left
  - (e) the reasons for seeking protection **have ceased to exist** (change of country conditions)
- s.108(4): (e) does not apply where there are **compelling reasons** from previous persecution, torture, treatment or punishment.
- Allowed application -> claim deemed rejected (s.108(3)).

## Consequences (H)
| Person | Ground | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Protected person (not yet PR) | any 108(1) | Inadmissible s.40.1(1); MD can issue a **departure order** (R228(1)(b.1)) |
| **Permanent resident** | **108(1)(a)-(d)** | **Loses PR on the final determination** (s.46(1)(c.1)) and is inadmissible (s.40.1(2)) - no IAD appeal on the cessation itself |
| Permanent resident | 108(1)(e) only | Not listed in s.46(1)(c.1)/40.1(2) -> PR status not lost on that basis (reading of the text; verify) (M) |
- Cessation decisions: Federal Court JR only (lawyer).
- Family members who got PR as dependants may be affected differently - assess each person.

## Vacation (s.109) (H)
- Minister applies to the RPD to **vacate** a positive decision obtained by **misrepresenting or withholding material facts**.
- RPD may refuse if other sufficient evidence considered at the original hearing justified protection (s.109(2)).
- Allowed -> claim deemed rejected and the decision nullified (s.109(3)). **PR lost** on final determination (s.46(1)(d)).
- Separately: misrepresentation (s.40) and loss of citizenship if later naturalized.

## Danger opinions (s.115(2)) (H)
- Non-refoulement (s.115(1)): a protected person or Convention refugee recognized by another country **shall not be removed** to a country of risk of persecution, torture, or cruel and unusual treatment.
- **Exceptions (s.115(2)):** (a) inadmissible for **serious criminality** and, in the Minister's opinion, a **danger to the public in Canada**; or (b) inadmissible on **security, human/international rights or organized criminality** and should not remain given the nature and severity of the acts or danger to security.
- Process: notice of intent to seek an opinion, disclosure, written submissions on danger, risk on return, H&C factors; Minister's delegate balances. JR to Federal Court (lawyer) (M on process detail).
- **PRRA limits (s.112(3)):** no refugee protection (only a stay assessment on s.97 factors plus danger/security, s.113(d)) for those inadmissible on security/rights/organized crime, serious criminality (10-year max offence conviction), Article 1F exclusion, or named in a security certificate.
- PRRA waiting periods: 12 months (36 for some) after a negative claim/PRRA (s.112(2)(b.1)-(c)).

## Consultant pitfalls
- **Ask every protected person/refugee-PR about trips home and home-country passports** before citizenship or PR card renewal - travel history appears in those files and in CBSA entry data, and can trigger cessation.
- Advise clients: do not apply for/use the home passport or travel to the home country before citizenship (risk assessment case by case; lawyer for any notice).
- Citizenship applications by refugee-origin PRs: screen for re-availment first (`07-citizenship-pr-status/citizenship-grant.md`).
- Any **cessation, vacation or danger-opinion notice -> escalate to a lawyer immediately** (Federal Court likely; RCIC scope limits).
