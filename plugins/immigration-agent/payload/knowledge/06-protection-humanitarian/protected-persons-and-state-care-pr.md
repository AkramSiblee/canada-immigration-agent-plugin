# PR for protected persons and for people who were in state care

## Must cover
- Protected person PR: who, how, Quebec, DFN wait
- State care public policy: eligibility, exemptions, deadline

## Current rules
### Permanent residence for protected persons (open; pages 2026-10-08)
- **Who can apply:** IRB accepted the claim; positive PRRA with protected person status (in most cases); or a protected temporary resident (entered on a TRP for urgent protection).
- **Cannot apply if:** also a Convention refugee in another country where the person could live, or a citizen, national or PR of a country other than the country of persecution.
- **Designated foreign nationals:** must wait **5 to 6 years** after the final decision. Applications filed earlier are returned.
- **How:** PR Portal, with document checklist **IMM 5286**. Paid representative: IMM 5476. The applicant must personally type their name on the declaration.
- **Fee:** $660 principal (no RPRF), per `11-forms-fees-processing/fee-schedule.md`.
- **List all dependants** when applying.
- **Quebec:** apply for a CSQ as soon as the claim or PRRA is accepted. IRCC doesn't need a CSQ if status came from the IRB. It **does** need one if status came from a positive PRRA. Non-protected family living in Quebec need a CSQ. A CSQ lasts 24 months, or until IRCC decides if a PR application is in process.
- Withdrawing the PR application doesn't remove protected person status.

### Pathway for people who were in state care (open until 2027-01-21)
- Public policy in effect 2024-01-22 to **2027-01-21**. Page dated 2026-03-05 shows no extension.
- **State care** means under the legal responsibility of a child and family services provider, with the provincial or territorial authority holding full parental responsibilities by court order.
- **Must:**
  - have come to Canada before age 19
  - have lived in Canada continuously for at least 3 years before applying (and since age 19, if now over 19)
  - have been under the legal responsibility of the provider at a designated child protection ministry for **at least 1 year, cumulative**
  - be physically in Canada when applying and when PR is granted
  - hold a valid passport, travel document, identity document or statutory declaration
- **Excluded:** serious reasons to consider crimes against peace, war crimes, serious non-political crimes outside Canada, or acts contrary to UN purposes (Article 1F).
- **Inadmissibility exceptions:** can still apply if inadmissible for serious criminality, criminality, excessive demand, financial reasons, misrepresentation tied to entry or overstay and unauthorized work or study, being sponsored by someone inadmissible for misrepresentation, non-compliance, or an inadmissible family member (unless under A34(1), A35(1) or A37(1)).
- **Family:** spouse or partner, dependent child, dependent child of a dependent child, in Canada. **All non-accompanying family must be declared, or they can't be sponsored later.**
- **Fee: none.**
- **Forms:** document checklist IMM 0203; IMM 0008 (Program "Other"; Category "In-Canada - Humanitarian & Compassionate Considerations"); IMM 5669; IMM 5406 if applicable; attestation letter and attendance records; photo for each person. Guide IMM 0204. Sent to the Humanitarian Migration Office in Vancouver (M: guide extract).
- **Removal:** a removal order is stayed once approved in principle (M: guide extract).
- **Quebec:** application is referred to MIFI, and a CSQ is needed.
- **TRP alternative:** a temporary resident permit is available to this group. It stays valid until its expiry or PR approval.

### Family abroad: the one-year window
| Situation | Rule | Clock |
|---|---|---|
| **Protected person in Canada** (IRPR s.176) | Family member included in the PR application, outside Canada when it was made, applies to an officer outside Canada | **Within 1 year after the applicant becomes a PR** |
| **Resettled refugee** (IRPR s.141) | Family member included in the application, or added before departure | **Within 1 year of refugee protection being conferred** |
- Family must not be inadmissible. For s.176, the bar is limited to the A21(2) grounds.
- Resettled refugees: IMM 5571 and guide IMM 5578, filed through the PR Portal; family use Guide 6000, IMM 0008, IMM 5669, IMM 5406.
- **An undeclared family member loses this route** and may be excluded from later sponsorship (IRPR 117(9)(d)). Declare everyone, even those not travelling.

### Losing protection: cessation and vacation
**Cessation (IRPA s.108), on the Minister's application to the RPD**, for:
- (a) voluntarily reavailing of the protection of the country of nationality
- (b) voluntarily reacquiring nationality
- (c) acquiring a new nationality and enjoying its protection
- (d) voluntarily re-establishing in the country
- (e) the reasons for protection have ceased to exist (exception in s.108(4): compelling reasons from previous persecution)

**Consequences:**
- Claim is deemed rejected (s.108(3)).
- Foreign national: inadmissible on a final determination (s.40.1(1)).
- **Permanent resident: on grounds (a) to (d), PR status is lost and the person is inadmissible** (ss.40.1(2), 46(1)(c.1)). Ground (e) doesn't remove PR status.
- *Galindo Camayo*, 2022 FCA 50: reavailment has three elements: voluntariness, intention and actual reavailment. Getting or renewing a home-country passport and using it raises a presumption, which can be rebutted. Outcomes are fact-dependent, so read the decision.

**Vacation (s.109):** RPD may vacate if protection was obtained by misrepresenting or withholding material facts. May be refused if other sufficient evidence justified protection. Decision is nullified (s.109(3)); PR is lost (s.46(1)(d)).

**Advise every protected person and refugee who is a PR:** don't travel to the home country or apply for or use its passport without advice first, **until they are Canadian citizens**. Cessation and vacation applications go to counsel, and check licence class before appearing at the RPD.

## Consultant pitfalls
- **State care deadline is 2027-01-21.** Get the attestation letter from the child welfare authority early.
- These clients are often vulnerable, and may have criminal or overstay history. Check that each ground falls within the listed exceptions.
- Bars and A34, A35 or A37 issues, and any removal matter, go to a lawyer.
- PRRA-based protected persons living in Quebec need a CSQ; IRB-based do not.
- Undeclared family: flag IRPR 117(9)(d) risk.
- Home-country passport renewal or a family visit can end PR status under s.108(1)(a).

## Common refusal reasons
- State care: under 1 year in care, no continuous residence, not in Canada at grant, excluded inadmissibility
- Protected person: status in a third country; application before DFN wait ends; missing dependants
