# Refugee protection: legal tests, guidelines, hearing preparation

## Must cover
- s.96 and s.97, exclusion, state protection, IFA, Chairperson's guidelines, hearing preparation, minors

## Current rules
**Claims are decided by the IRB. Check your licence class before appearing (`10-consultant-practice/scope-and-representation.md`). Federal Court work and retroactive bars go to a lawyer.**

### The two grounds
| | Convention refugee (IRPA s.96) | Person in need of protection (s.97) |
|---|---|---|
| Harm | Well-founded fear of persecution | Danger of torture, or risk to life or of cruel and unusual treatment or punishment |
| Link | **Race, religion, nationality, particular social group or political opinion** | No ground needed; risk must be **personal** |
| Standard of risk | **Serious possibility or reasonable chance** | **More likely than not** (*Li*, FCA) |
| Limits | Must be unable or, because of the fear, unwilling to seek protection of each country of nationality | Unable or unwilling to get state protection; risk in every part of the country; **not faced generally by others**; not from lawful sanctions; not from inadequate health care |

### Elements officers and members test
- **Well-founded fear** (*Ward*, SCC): both subjective fear and an objective basis.
- **State protection:** states are presumed able to protect. The claimant must rebut this; the inquiry is contextual, not done in a vacuum.
- **Internal flight alternative:** two-pronged. A viable part of the country, and not unreasonable for this claimant to go there. Needs a distinct assessment of the region, considering the claimant's identity.
- **Each country of nationality** must be addressed.
- **Change of circumstances and compelling reasons:** IRB paper, Chapter 7; s.108(4).
- Standard of proof for facts: balance of probabilities.

### Exclusion (IRPA s.98)
- **Article 1E:** status in a third country substantially like nationals. Aimed at asylum shopping (*Zeng*, FCA). Standard on the status question: balance of probabilities.
- **Article 1F:** serious reasons for considering crimes against peace, war crimes, crimes against humanity; serious non-political crime outside Canada before admission; acts contrary to UN purposes. Includes complicity.
- Criminal or war crimes issues go to a lawyer.

### Chairperson's guidelines (IRB page; numbers dropped from titles 2026-11-03, content unchanged)
Members are expected to apply them or explain why not.

| Guideline | Use it for |
|---|---|
| **Gender considerations** (2022, amended 2023-10-31) | Avoiding myths and stereotypes; trauma-informed adjudication; all genders |
| **Sexual orientation, gender identity and expression, sex characteristics** (2017, revised 2021) | Sensitive questioning; SOGIESC evidence |
| **Minors** (2023-10-31) | All under 18; best interests of the child; designated representatives; eliciting evidence |
| **Accessibility: procedural accommodations** (2023-10-31) | Disability, vulnerability, personal characteristics. Replaced the "vulnerable persons" guideline; no label needed |
| **Changing date or time of a proceeding** | Postponements in all divisions |
| **Detention** | Long-term detention, danger to the public, alternatives |

### Preparing for an RPD hearing
- **Timelines (SOR/2026-219):** personal documents within 30 days of referral; country documents up to 10 days before the hearing; changes to basis of claim information within 5 days of the notice to appear. See `09-procedure-litigation/irb-divisions-and-appeals.md`.
- **Keep contact details current** with RPD, IRCC and CBSA. A missed notice can lead to abandonment, and then a likely order to leave.
- **National Documentation Packages** are the IRB's country evidence. Check the current package for the country, and address it.
- **Hearing Ready Tours:** free virtual IRB sessions on evidence and hearing day.
- Basis of claim information is declared accurate, complete and truthful. Inconsistencies with earlier statements (port of entry, visa forms) are a common credibility issue. Reconcile them first.
- Request accommodations or a designated representative early.

### Unaccompanied minors
- Exempt from the C-12 bars (`refugee-claims-inland.md`).
- A designated representative is required for minors and incapable persons (SOR/2026-218).
- Resettled minors: Guardianship Protocol. Family reunification first, and long-term care arrangements if resettled.

## Recent changes (2025-2027)
- 2026-11-03: IRB guidelines lost their numbers; older citations ("Guideline 4", "Guideline 9") still refer to the same documents (H)
- 2023-10-31: Minors and Accessibility guidelines replaced older versions (H)

## Consultant pitfalls
- s.96 and s.97 have different risk standards. Argue both where possible.
- Generalized risk defeats s.97. Show what makes the risk personal.
- Don't cite the old "vulnerable persons" guideline.
- RPD Rules changed 2026-11-03. Older checklists on BOC Forms are out of date for new claims.

## Common refusal reasons
- Credibility; no nexus; state protection not rebutted; viable IFA; generalized risk; exclusion

## Re-verify before advising
- IRB Legal Services papers are general guides. Read the cited case before relying on it
- IRB practice notices after C-12
- 2024-2026 RPD and RAD decisions need a manual CanLII search
