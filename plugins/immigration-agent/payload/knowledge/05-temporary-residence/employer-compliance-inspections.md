# Employer compliance and inspections (TFWP and IMP)

## Must cover
- Who inspects, triggers, lookback, employer conditions (IRPR 209.2 IMP / 209.3 TFWP), justifications, procedure, AMPs and bans (IRPR Schedule 2), publication, worker permit revocation

## Current rules
### Who inspects (IRCC bulletin, page 2026-09-18) (H)
- **IMP (LMIA-exempt) employers: IRCC** staff. Referral to IRCC's Risk and Integrity Authority Branch, Compliance Inspections and Investigations Division; on-site visits by Immigration Operations.
- **TFWP (LMIA) employers: ESDC / Service Canada.**
- Covers employers whose offers led to permits issued **on or after 2013-12-31**.

### Triggers (IRPR 209.5 / 209.6)
1. Reason to suspect non-compliance (complaints, ESDC/CBSA, unions, NGOs, provinces, media, voluntary disclosure)
2. Past non-compliance
3. **Random selection** (ESDC publishes an Algorithmic Impact Assessment)
4. Emergencies Act / Quarantine Act order
5. Communicable disease at the workplace
- ESDC 2025-26 focus: high-risk sectors; food services named a high-risk review sector (ESDC 2026-07-09; see work-permits-lmia.md).

### Lookback and records
- Inspection can start from the worker's first day of employment **up to 6 years** after.
- Employer must **keep all records 6 years** from the first day of the employment period on the permit.
- On-site, announced or unannounced; warrantless except private dwellings (ESDC page 2026-05-12). Interviews with consent; photos/audio/video with written consent. Third parties (banks, payroll, accountants, recruiters) can be asked for documents.
- **Refusing to cooperate is itself a Type C violation** (R209.4(1)).

### IMP employer conditions (IRPR 209.2)
1. Remain **actively engaged in the business** in the offer
2. Give the worker the **rights information** (both official languages) on or before day 1
3. Comply with federal/provincial **employment and recruitment laws**
4. **Same occupation**; wages and working conditions **substantially the same as, but not less favourable than**, the offer
5. Reasonable efforts for a workplace **free of abuse** (physical, sexual, psychological, financial) and reprisal
6. Reasonable efforts to give **access to health care** if injured or ill at work
7. **No charging or recovering** the compliance fee or recruitment fees (also ensure recruiters don't)
8. Offer information accurate; **prove it for 6 years**; keep documents
- Employment agreement (R209.11(1)(e)) in the worker's chosen official language, signed by both, given to the worker (attested since May 2024).
- Duty changes need a **new offer and new permit first**.

### TFWP employer conditions (IRPR 209.3; ESDC page 2026-05-12)
- **29 conditions**, incl. all of the above plus: meet LMIA decision letter and annex terms; LMIA commitments (jobs, hiring, training, transition plan, skills transfer); accurate LMIA information; adequate housing (SAWP); live-in caregiver extras.
- Wage increases up to inflation may be acceptable. Report changes or errors to the **Employer Contact Centre 1-800-367-5693**.

### Justifications (R209.2(3), (3.1), (4); R209.4(2))
- Change in law; change in collective agreement; economic conditions affecting all employees equally; good-faith interpretation error **with compensation**; unintentional accounting/administrative error **with compensation**; similar circumstances; force majeure. Compensation must be shown **before** the preliminary finding. (List from bulletin summary portion: M on wording.)

### Procedure
- Inspection letter (trigger, documents, deadline) -> initial finding, chance to justify -> **Notice of Preliminary Finding (NOPF)**: violations, points, proposed sanction; **30 days to respond** (extension case by case) -> **Notice of Final Determination (NOFD)**.
- Extrinsic evidence must be disclosed in the NOPF.
- **Voluntary disclosure:** IMM 5964. If accepted, **4 points subtracted** (floor 0).

### Penalties: points, AMPs, bans (IRPR Schedule 2, laws-lois page 2026-11-02; IRCC consequences page 2024-07-19) (H)
- Violation types: **A** = records accuracy/retention; **B** = employment law, occupation/wages/conditions, rights information; **C** = abuse, health care, fee recovery, not actively engaged, failure to cooperate.
- Points = compliance history (1st violation: A/B 1, C 2; 2nd: A 2, B 2, C 3; 3rd+: A 2, B 3, C 4; **no expiry**) + severity modifiers (benefit 0-6, abuse 0-10, economic harm 0-6, failure to remediate 0-3, failure to prevent recurrence 0-3).
- **Under 2 points: warning letter** (not published).
- Small business = 100 or fewer employees **or** $5M or less gross revenue; large = more than 100 employees **and** more than $5M.

- AMPs are per violation; **total cap $1M per NOFD** (12-month look-back). Bans are not cumulative; the longest applies. Violations before 2015-12-01: flat 2-year ban.
- Ban applies to **both TFWP and IMP**. Pending LMIAs refused, fees not refunded; previous LMIAs can be suspended or revoked.
- Name, address, violations and penalty **published** on the IRCC non-compliant employers list (live page; check the page itself). AMP without ban: employer stays eligible unless the AMP is unpaid.
- Worker impact: work permits of workers at a non-compliant employer **may be revoked** under Ministerial Instructions, depending on severity (bulletin; 2024-07-26 update). Vulnerable workers: see open-work-permits.md (VWOWP).

### IRPA s.124 offence and consequences of unauthorized work (IRPA laws-lois page-16, current to 2026-10-29, last amended 2026-03-26; read 2026-11-17) (H)
- **s.124(1)(c):** offence for **every person** who employs a foreign national in a capacity the person is not authorized under IRPA to be employed in. Covers recruiters and agencies, not only the payroll employer (CBSA 2026-04-13: agency owner convicted).
- **s.124(2) deemed knowledge:** a person who fails to exercise **due diligence** to determine whether the employment is authorized is **deemed to know** it is not authorized. In practice, due diligence is the employer's defence.
- **s.124(3) is NOT an employer defence:** it covers only persons under **s.148(1) (transporters)** charged under **s.124(1)(a)**. Don't cite it for employers.
- **s.125 penalties (hybrid offence, per count):** indictment: fine up to **$50,000** and/or up to **2 years**; summary: fine up to **$10,000** and/or up to **6 months**.
- **Related:** s.131 (knowingly inducing, aiding or abetting a s.124 offence, e.g., a recruiter or HR staff); s.126/127 misrepresentation, penalty under s.128 up to **$100,000 / 5 years** on indictment.
- **2026 amendments:** IRPA last amended 2026-03-26 (Bill C-12 Royal Assent reported 2026-03-27, secondary). The page shows **no amendment history on ss.124-125**, so the fines are unchanged. (H on text; C-12 date M)
- **Two separate tracks:**
  - **AMPs/bans (IRPR Schedule 2):** administrative. Run by IRCC (IMP) or ESDC (TFWP) against employers bound by R209.2/209.3 conditions. Civil standard. See the tables above.
  - **s.124 prosecution:** criminal. CBSA Criminal Investigations lays charges and the Crown must prove the case. Applies to any employer, including ones that never used the TFWP or IMP (e.g., hiring visitors or out-of-status people). Both tracks can apply to the same facts.
- **Recent CBSA cases:**
  - 2026-04-13 (page date): Banff/Jasper hotels case. Agency owner pleaded guilty 2026-03-02 to 2 counts under s.124(1)(c) and a s.131 count. Sentence: **$70,000 fine**, 2 years' probation, 50 hours' community service. His second s.124(1)(c) conviction. (H)
  - 2026-02-27: Granby QC campground. Directors charged with 1 count under s.124(1)(c) for **21 workers**, offences May 2022 to April 2024; employee charged with 2 counts under s.131. CBSA: "Anyone who hires foreign workers without authorization is subject to criminal prosecution." (H)
  - Earlier cases (secondary search, CBSA releases not opened): 2025 Winnipeg construction manager, $50,000 fine plus 20-month conditional sentence; 2025 Ontario (OPP joint), three businesses fined, 700+ workers; 2016 Edmonton, 18 months' jail for 68 workers. (M)
  - Report tips: CBSA Border Watch Line **1-888-502-9060**.
- **What due diligence looks like (practice; no IRCC/CBSA checklist found) (M):**
  - See the original permit (not just a copy) and check each condition against the job: **employer name** (employer-specific), **occupation**, **location**, **expiry date**, and any restrictions (e.g., "not authorized to work in childcare", medical restrictions).
  - **Students:** off-campus work only if the permit allows it and the student is enrolled full time at the named DLI. **Up to 24 h/week in term**, full time only in scheduled breaks. A drop to part time or a leave means no off-campus work. Keep timesheets across all jobs if possible (see study-permit-status-and-compliance.md, read 2026-11-17, H).
  - **Maintained status (R183(5)-(6)):** worker may keep working **under the same conditions** if an extension was filed **before expiry**. Get proof of submission date and time. No change of employer until the new permit issues (except a valid PPCHANGEWORK2020 filing). Ends if the worker leaves Canada or on refusal (see trp-restoration-maintained-status.md).
  - **Restoration:** **no work** until restoration and the new permit are approved (see study-permit-status-and-compliance.md, H).
  - **Visitors / eTA holders:** no work unless R186 lists the activity as no-permit work (see work-permit-process-and-status.md).
  - Diarize expiry dates. Re-check at each renewal. Keep copies for 6 years (also the R209 record rule).
  - Get a SIN starting with 9 and check its expiry (supporting evidence only, not proof of authorization) (M).
- **Employer consequences:**
  - Criminal record and fine/jail (s.125).
  - Schedule 2 AMPs and bans if the employer is bound by R209.2/209.3 conditions.
  - **Workers can't get a permit for an ineligible employer:** R200(3)(h) bars a permit where the employer is ineligible under R209.95(1)(b) or in default of an AMP, including a missed payment agreement (R200, current to 2026-10-29) (H).
  - Ineligible employers are published on the non-compliant list; pending LMIAs are refused.
  - Unknown: whether a s.124 conviction alone makes the employer ineligible or counts in the LMIA genuineness assessment. No provision found linking them; treat as a risk factor.
- **Worker consequences:**
  - **R200(3)(e):** no work permit if the person did **unauthorized work or study** or breached a prior condition, **unless 6 months have passed since it stopped**; or the breach was only of R185(a), 185(b)(i)-(iii) or 185(c) (restorable conditions); or R206 applies; or a TRP was later issued. R200(3.1) exempts R207.1(1) vulnerable-worker cases (R200 current to 2026-10-29) (H). Same bar applies to a PGWP.
  - Unauthorized work breaches R183(1). Risk of **A41 inadmissibility**, an A44 report and a removal order (see 09-procedure-litigation removals file). Can also feed **A40 misrepresentation** if not disclosed later. (H on R200; removal pathway M)
  - Victims of abuse or exploitation: consider the VWOWP (open-work-permits.md).
- **Escalate:** any client charged or under CBSA investigation for s.124/131 goes to a **criminal lawyer** (outside RCIC scope).

### Enforcement figures (ESDC release 2026-07-09, primary read 2026-11-20) (H)
- TFWP 2025-04-01 to 2026-03-31: **1,488 inspections finalized** (risk-targeted), **12% non-compliant**, **AMPs over $10.2M** (2024-25: $4.5M), **30 employers banned**.
- Examples in the release: Manitoba long-haul trucking $240,000 + 5-yr ban (working conditions); Quebec technical consulting $122,000 + 5-yr ban (not employed in the stated occupation); Nova Scotia restaurant $126,000 + 2-yr ban (wages).
- Ceiling: AMPs up to **$1 million per year** plus temporary or permanent bans. Release also restates the Low-Wage **8 consecutive weeks** advertising rule (up from 4).
- Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/news/2026/07/the-government-of-canada-highlights-doubling-of-compliance-monetary-penalties-under-the-temporary-foreign-worker-program.html
- IMP 2024-25 (secondary): 4,328 inspections, 55 penalties. Don't mix the two programs.

## Consultant pitfalls
- Most common real-world breach: **wage or duty drift** (raise withheld, new title, different worksite, reduced hours). Any change to occupation needs a new offer and permit before the change.
- **Recruitment fee recovery** (directly or through a recruiter) is Type C: high points fast. Ask how the worker was recruited.
- Keep a **6-year compliance binder** per worker: offer/LMIA, signed agreement, payroll, timesheets, rights-information proof, duty descriptions.
- Respond to the NOPF within 30 days with compensation proof; compensation shown after the preliminary finding may not count.
- Consider **voluntary disclosure (IMM 5964)** before an inspection: -4 points.
- An employer ban kills pending LMIAs and IMP offers: run a compliance check at intake for employer clients (see work-permit-process-and-status.md, non-compliant employers list).
- Representing the employer and the worker in the same inspection can be a conflict (see 10-consultant-practice/advertising-and-conflicts.md).
- Penalties are reviewable only by Federal Court JR: refer to a lawyer.
