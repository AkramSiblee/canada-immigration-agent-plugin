# Federal Court judicial review

## Must cover
- Leave deadlines (15/60 days), standards, process; RCIC cannot appear; refer to lawyer

## Current rules
- **Leave required** (IRPA s.72(1)). Appeal rights under IRPA (e.g. IAD, RAD) must be exhausted first (s.72(2)(a)).
- **Deadline** (s.72(2)(b)): **15 days** for a matter arising **in Canada**; **60 days** outside Canada, from notification or awareness. Extension only "for special reasons" (s.72(2)(c)).
- If leave is granted, the hearing is 30-90 days later (s.74(b)). Appeal to the FCA only on a **certified question** (s.74(d)).
- **Standard of review**: reasonableness presumed (*Canada (MCI) v Vavilov*, 2019 SCC 65); correctness for limited categories. Procedural fairness is reviewed on a fairness standard.
- **Representation: lawyers only** (Federal Court Rules; RCICs cannot appear). **RCIC role**: identify the deadline, preserve the file, and **refer immediately** (CLAUDE.md rule 4).
- Remedy: usually the decision is set aside and **sent back for redetermination**. The court doesn't grant the visa.
- Stays of removal: separate motion (lawyer); tripartite test (serious issue, irreparable harm, balance of convenience).

## Recent changes (2025-2027)
- IRPA last amended 2026-03-26. No change to s.72-74 (H)

## Consultant pitfalls
- The **15-day** in-Canada clock is very short. Count from the date the client learned of the decision. Calendar it at intake.
- Request GCMS in parallel, but don't wait for it.
- Compare reapplication vs JR: JR fixes errors of law or unreasonableness. Reapplication fixes weak evidence.

## Common refusal reasons
- Leave denied (no arguable case); out of time

## Re-verify before advising
- Federal Court Citizenship, Immigration and Refugee Protection Rules (SOR/93-22) amendments; FC practice guidelines
