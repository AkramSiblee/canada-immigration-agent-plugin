# Immigration Division: A44 reports, admissibility hearings, detention

IRPA current to 2026-10-29, last amended 2026-03-26 (2026, c. 4 - Strengthening Canada's Immigration System and Borders Act); IRPR last amended 2026-10-23.

## Must cover
- A44 report path, who decides (officer/Minister's delegate vs ID), ID outcomes, detention grounds, review timing, release conditions, minors, representation

## A44 report -> who decides (H)
1. Officer writes an **A44(1) report** (believes a PR or FN in Canada is inadmissible) -> Minister's delegate (MD).
2. MD either:
   - **makes the removal order itself** in prescribed cases (IRPR **228**) - FN only, e.g. criminality/serious criminality (deportation), FN cessation s.40.1(1) (departure), s.41 non-compliance cases (overstay, work/study without authorization, no visa...), family member cases, sanctions; **PR residency-obligation** failures (departure order, R228(2)); certain refugee claimants (departure, R228(3)); or
   - **refers to the Immigration Division** for an admissibility hearing (all other cases, incl. **every PR except residency**, misrepresentation, security, organized crime, medical/financial).
- R228(4): MD cannot make the order for unaccompanied minors or persons unable to appreciate the proceedings -> ID.
- A44(3): officer or ID may impose conditions (incl. deposit/guarantee).
- **New s.44.1 (2026, c. 4):** ID must not hold an admissibility hearing if the person is **not physically present in Canada**.
- **Opportunity to respond:** the MD/officer process is where submissions (H&C context, discretion not to write/refer the report) are made. Act early - once referred, the ID has no H&C jurisdiction.

## ID admissibility hearing (s.45) (H)
- Outcomes: recognize right to enter; grant PR/TR status if requirements met; authorize entry for further examination; or **make the applicable removal order** (`removals-cbsa-deferrals.md` for order types).
- Minister's counsel (CBSA hearings officer) presents the case. Standard for ss.34-37 is "reasonable grounds to believe" (s.33).
- Appeal: **IAD** for PRs, protected persons and visa holders, except where barred by s.64 (security, rights, sanctions, serious criminality with 6+ months imposed, organized crime). Otherwise Federal Court JR (lawyer).

## Detention (H)
- **Grounds (s.55, s.58(1)):** danger to the public; unlikely to appear (flight risk); identity not established (with lack of cooperation / Minister's efforts); Minister taking steps on a reasonable suspicion of security, rights, sanctions, serious criminality, criminality or organized crime. **Designated foreign nationals 16+: mandatory detention** (s.55(3.1)).
- **Release by officer** before first review if reasons no longer exist (s.56).
- **Reviews (s.57):** ID within **48 hours**, then within **7 days**, then **every 30 days**. Early review possible on changed circumstances. DFN: first within **14 days**, then every **6 months** (s.57.1).
- **ID "shall order the release"** unless a s.58(1) ground is made out; the **onus is always on the Minister** (Guideline).
- **Factors:** flight risk R245 (past compliance, strong community ties...); danger R246 (violent/sexual/weapons convictions, smuggling, trafficking...); identity R247 (cooperation, fraudulent documents); R248 other factors **always considered**: reason and length of detention, likely future length, unexplained delays, **alternatives to detention**, **best interests of a directly affected child** (R248.1 factors).
- **Minors:** detained only as a last resort (s.60); R249 special considerations.
- **Chairperson's guideline: Detention** (formerly Guideline 2; guidelines renumbered/untitled from 2026-11-03; version amended 2021-04):
  - detention is exceptional; cannot be indefinite;
  - first consider release on **minimum conditions without a deposit/guarantee**;
  - conditions must be tailored, proportionate, attainable;
  - reassess alternatives at every review; reasons must explain why ATD rejected;
  - **CBSA ATD program**: community case management and supervision, voice reporting, electronic monitoring (exceptional).
- **Bonds:** **deposit** (cash held by government) or **guarantee** (performance bond). Bondsperson: friend, family, employer, community worker, even the detainee; a **guarantor must be a citizen or PR**. Amount proportionate to risk.
- Typical conditions: address and CBSA reporting, curfew, live with bondsperson, no alcohol/drugs, no criminal offences, present for removal.
- Hearings **virtual by default**; in person on written request.

## Representation (H)
- Detainee may self-represent, hire a lawyer or **licensed immigration consultant** (RCIC-IRB class for paid representation; s.91), notary in Quebec. Unpaid friend/family may assist.
- **Designated representative** must be appointed for minors and persons unable to appreciate the proceedings.
- Federal Court JR of detention/ID decisions: lawyer (escalate).

## Consultant pitfalls
- Calendar the 48h / 7d / 30d cycle the same day the client is detained. Line up bondsperson evidence (ID, income, relationship, residence) before the 48-hour review.
- A PR facing an A44 report for criminality: make submissions to the MD before referral; consider IAD rights (lost if 6+ months imposed).
- Do not let a client sign a "voluntary departure" or concede inadmissibility without assessing IAD/PRRA consequences.
- Detained refugee claimant: check C-12 eligibility and PRRA timing (`refugee-claims-inland.md`, `stca-prra.md`).
