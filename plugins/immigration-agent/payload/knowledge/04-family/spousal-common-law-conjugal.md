# Spouse, common-law and conjugal sponsorship

## Must cover
- Outside Canada vs in-Canada class; open work permit; IAD rights
- Genuineness (IRPR s.4), evidence, interviews, common refusals, fees

## Current rules
**Status: OPEN** (IRCC, 2026-03-05). Not affected by the PGP pause.

### Relationships (IRPR s.1(1), s.2)
| Type | Test |
|---|---|
| Spouse | Legally married (valid where celebrated **and** in Canada); 18+; genuine |
| Common-law | Cohabiting in a conjugal relationship for **12 consecutive months** (s.1(1)); s.1(2) covers persecution/penal control |
| Conjugal | Outside Canada; conjugal relationship for at least 1 yr; **cannot** live together or marry because of legal, immigration, social, cultural or religious barriers |

### Two classes
| | Family class (s.117(1)(a)) | Spouse or common-law partner in Canada class (ss.123-124) |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Applicant usually outside Canada (can be inside) | Applicant **in Canada, cohabiting** with sponsor, **with TR status** (status policy exists for out-of-status spouses) |
| Conjugal partners | Yes | No |
| Appeal on refusal | **IAD appeal** (IRPA s.63(1)) | **No IAD**; only JR (lawyer) |
| Travel during processing | Free | Leaving Canada risks the cohabitation requirement |

### Sponsor (IRCC eligibility page; IRPR s.130, s.133)
- 18+; citizen, PR or registered Indian; lives in Canada (a citizen abroad must show intent to return; a **PR abroad cannot sponsor**)
- No income test for spouse, partner or child (s.133(4)) unless the sponsored person has a dependent child who has a child of their own
- Bars: see sponsor-eligibility-and-bars.md
- **Undertaking: 3 years** from PR (s.132(1)(b)(i)). Quebec: separate MIFI undertaking.

### Open work permit (public policy; IRCC page 2026-04-17)
- Eligible: a spouse, common-law or conjugal partner **living in Canada with the sponsor**, included in a PR application with an **AOR**; also an accompanying dependent child of the principal applicant.
- No AOR needed only if the permit/status expires in **2 weeks or less**.
- Not available at a port of entry, or if the PR application was refused, withdrawn or returned.
- Extension: up to 2 more years while PR is in process.

- **OWP applies to both classes (policy 2023-05-26; page 2026-04-17, read 2026-11-09):** family class applicants living in Canada with the sponsor, with status, qualify on the same terms as SCLPC applicants. Permit up to 2 years. So work rights don't by themselves favour inland; compare processing (18 v 26 months), IAD and travel.

### Fees (since 2026-04-30)
- $1,260 (sponsorship $90 + principal $570 + RPRF $600). Dependent child $180. OWP $255 ($155 + $100). Biometrics $85.

### Processing
- About **15 months** (decisions Sep 2025-Aug 2026, IRCC inventory page). The 2027 humanitarian cohort aims for "closer to 12 months" outside Quebec.
- Spouses/partners/children target 66,000 per year (2027-2029).
- IRCC estimate for a new application (2026-10-09): outside Canada about **18 months** (QC 33); in Canada about **26 months** (QC 32) (H). Plan the class choice with this in mind.

### Evidence notes (Guide 5289 page 2026-04-23, read 2026-11-09) (H)
- Marriage certificate or proof of registration required; a record of solemnization or licence isn't accepted. Proof of communication capped at **10 pages** and must be translated. Letter of explanation can be uploaded in optional documents. A married couple can't use the common-law category (who-you-can-sponsor page 2026-09-11). IMM 5533 checklist couldn't be retrieved this pass; check it before filing.
- Processing estimates reconfirmed 2026-10-09 data: outside Canada 18 months (QC 33); in Canada 26 months (QC 32).

### Genuineness factors (read 2026-11-09) (M)
- IRB Ch. 6 (2008) quotes *Chavez* (IAD TA3-24409, 2005): intent, length of relationship, time together, conduct at meeting, engagement and wedding, knowledge of each other, contact, support, extended family. **No single criterion is decisive**; age difference may be considered (*Froment*; *Khera*). Its statement that showing one prong is enough predates the current disjunctive s.4(1): both prongs must be answered (IRB appeal page 2023-03-22 confirms). Verify citations and post-2008 cases before relying on them.
- IMM 5533 (09-2022 version retrieved): proof of contact max 10 pages; photos max 20; not living together needs proof of sponsor's visits or an explanation in IMM 5532 Part C Q4; living together needs at least 2 document sets.

## Recent changes (2025-2027)
- **Correction to baseline:** the 2025-01-21 OWP restriction applied to **family OWPs for spouses of students and workers** (see 05-temporary-residence/open-work-permits.md). It did **not** restrict the in-Canada sponsorship OWP, which remains available as above (H).
- 2026-04-30: sponsorship fee $90; sponsored principal $570; RPRF $600 (H) - confirmed
- 2026-09-10: undeclared-family (117(9)(d)) public policy **ended**. Applications after that date get no relief (H)
- 2027: spousal inventory included in the time-limited humanitarian cohort (up to 100,000; outside Levels) (H)

## Consultant pitfalls
- **Choose the class carefully.** In-Canada gives an OWP but no IAD appeal. Family class has an appeal (s.63(1)), and the applicant can travel.
- **Genuineness (s.4):** the relationship must not be primarily for status **and** must be genuine (two separate tests). Prepare for interviews with consistent histories.
- **s.4.1:** reconciling with a former spouse after a status-driven divorce is a bar.
- **s.117(9)(c.1):** proxy, telephone or online marriages where a spouse was not physically present are not recognized (except CAF).
- **s.117(9)(b):** an existing undertaking for a previous spouse not yet ended is a bar.
- **s.117(9)(d) undeclared family:** the sponsor's own undeclared dependants are permanently barred. **The relief public policy ended 2026-09-10.**
- Misrepresentation leads to a s.40 bar plus an AMP (SOR/2026-220). An IAD appeal is still available for misrepresentation where the applicant is a spouse/partner/child (s.64(3)).
- Ghost consultants or "marriage brokers": flag them and use IMM 5476 for any representative.

## Common refusal reasons
- s.4 bad faith (limited cohabitation, inconsistent interviews, cultural deviations unexplained, short courtship tied to status expiry)
- Common-law 12-month cohabitation not proven (no joint lease, bills or ID)
- Conjugal: no proof that marriage or cohabitation is impossible
- Sponsor ineligible (s.130(3) 5-year bar; s.133 bars)
- Applicant inadmissible (criminality, medical, misrepresentation)
