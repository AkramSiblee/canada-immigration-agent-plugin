# Decision tree: visitor visa refusal

## Must cover
- Refusal grounds, what officers weigh, reapply vs JR

## Current rules
```
1. Read the refusal letter. Which ground?
   - Misrepresentation (A40): separate letter, 5-year ban -> stop; see 08-inadmissibility/misrepresentation-s40.md
   - Other inadmissibility (A34-42) -> see 08-inadmissibility; criminal equivalency -> lawyer
   - Not satisfied will leave (R179(b)) -> step 2
   - Incomplete or documents -> fix and reapply
2. Get the reasons: GCMS notes (ATIP or as provided with the letter)
   - Generic notes? Note the weakness for counsel
3. Map each stated concern to evidence:
   - Family ties in Canada vs outside -> list all family; caregiving; who stays behind
   - Purpose not consistent with temporary stay -> clear plan, dates, reason to return
   - Funds -> history, source, explain deposits, host support if used
   - Employment, assets -> letter granting leave, pension, property, business
   - Travel history, past compliance -> old passports, exit dates
   - Status in country of residence -> proof and expiry
4. Was the evidence already before the officer?
   - No, or weak -> reapply with new evidence (usually best)
   - Yes, and the officer ignored or misstated it -> JR option: refer to a lawyer now
5. Deadline check if JR is possible: 60 days (decision outside Canada) -> see 09-procedure-litigation
6. Reapplying: new application, new fee. Answer "yes" to previous refusal. Address each reason
7. Long stays with a child or grandchild in Canada? Consider Super Visa instead
```

### What officers weigh (PDI)
- Length of stay; means of support; obligations and ties to the home country; purpose and context; credibility of documents; past compliance, including biometric information sharing.
- Local economic or political conditions can be considered even when ties look strong.
- Past compliance is positive, non-compliance negative. **No history is neutral and not a ground for refusal** (stated in the PDI for spouses and partners).
- Entry and exit records are available to officers. Land records and air entries from 2019-02-26; air exits from June 2021.

### Parents and grandparents
- A pending or possible PR application does not by itself bar a visa. If the officer is satisfied on a balance of probabilities that they will leave, officers "will normally issue" a TRV.
- Bias example in the PDI: assuming applicants with PR applications want to overstay.

### Refusal grounds (PDI)
- Not satisfied the person will leave by the end of the authorized stay (R179)
- Failure to meet A11, A20(1)(b), or R179 requirements
- Inadmissibility: security, human rights, criminality, organized crime, health, financial, misrepresentation, non-compliance, inadmissible family member

## Recent changes (2025-2027)
- 2026: Federal Court set aside several generic family-ties refusals (`13-case-law/visitor-visa-refusals.md`) (H)

## Consultant pitfalls
- Reapplying with the same evidence usually repeats the result.
- Officers need not warn about weak evidence. Put everything in the first filing.
- Judicial review needs a lawyer; an RCIC can't represent there (`10-consultant-practice/scope-and-representation.md`).
- Never advise omitting a past refusal. Biometric and file checks will show it (A16(1), A40).

## Common refusal reasons
- R179(b): family ties, purpose, finances, employment, status abroad
