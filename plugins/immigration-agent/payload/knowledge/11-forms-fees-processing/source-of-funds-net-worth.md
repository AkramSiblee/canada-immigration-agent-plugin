# Source of funds and net worth: checklist for business clients

## Must cover
- Legally earned funds, gifts, loans, tracing, property valuation, third-party verification, currency, tax, s.40 risk
- Program-specific rules marked by province (NB, NS, BC, MB)

## Current rules
Confidence: NB, NS and BC rows are from the official guides (H for what they say). Manitoba could not be read (official site returned 403 on 2026-11-17): M/L. Items marked **[practice]** are professional practice, not a written rule.

### Checklist
1. **Full disclosure first.** Declare all assets and liabilities of the applicant and spouse (and dependants for BC). Hiding a liability or a co-owned asset is a misrepresentation risk even if the minimum is met. BC uses IRCC **Schedule 4A (Personal Net Worth)** plus the **Section J accumulation-of-wealth narrative**; NB uses NBBIS-002 (net worth) and NBBIS-003 (narrative of accumulated funds).
2. **Legally earned.** Tie each asset to a lawful source: employment income (pay records, tax returns), business profits (financial statements, corporate tax filings, dividend records), sale of property or shares (sale deeds, bank credits), investments. **[practice]** Cash-based business income without tax filings is the most common gap.
3. **Tracing period.** No official guide read sets a fixed "5-year" trace. Verifiers trace the **accumulation** of the declared net worth, often across the client's working life for large balances. **[practice]** Prepare 5+ years of statements for any account that holds key funds, plus the origin of any large deposit. Follow the verifier's own checklist (NS publishes a "Net Worth Verifier Document Checklist").
4. **Gifts.**
   - Gift deed or declaration: donor, recipient, amount, date, relationship, "no repayment" statement. **[practice]**
   - Donor's capacity: donor's income, tax filings, and the bank trail from donor to client. **[practice]**
   - Funds must sit in the applicant's (or spouse's) name. NB says net worth "must be in either or both of your names". **NB: gifts, donations and inheritances received less than 6 months before the application are not counted.**
   - BC: future inheritances cannot be counted. Past gifts are not excluded by the program guide, but they still need a legal source.
   - NS: no gift rule in the guide. Manitoba: unknown. Check the verifier's checklist.
5. **Loans and debts.** Net worth = assets minus liabilities (NB, BC definitions). Borrowed money adds nothing to net worth. Investment rules are stricter:
   - BC: the **minimum $200,000 eligible investment must come from personal funds**; external financing only above the minimum; mortgages and loans do not count toward personal investment. External financing and investing **> 50% of net worth** cost risk-factor points.
   - NS: the stated personal investment "must not include cash or loans"; external financing and > 50% of net worth are risk factors in plan review.
   - IRCC Express Entry settlement funds (different test): cannot be borrowed; property equity does not count (page modified 2026-10-16).
6. **Property valuations.** Show ownership (title deed) and current market value with supporting documents; deduct mortgages (BC: "equity amounts based on current market values"). **[practice]** Use a recent independent valuation; avoid valuations by relatives or interested parties. NS requires funds movable to Canada quickly, so heavy reliance on real estate is a risk.
7. **Business ownership interests.** Prove share percentage with legal documents (shareholder register, agreements). BC counts the current value of the ownership share (book value for scoring). NB requires purchase of an NB business at "proven fair market value".
8. **Third-party verification.** Use only the province's designated verifier, engaged **after** the ITA (NB, BC). The province keeps sole discretion and can ask for more (NB, BC). Do not let the verifier help with the business plan (NB refusal ground).
9. **Currency conversion.** All program figures are in CAD (BC). No guide read names an exchange-rate source. **[practice]** Use the Bank of Canada rate on a stated date, show the calculation, and keep a buffer for currency swings and verification timing. Unknown: which rate each verifier applies.
10. **Tax compliance.** Lawful funds should match tax filings in the source country. **[practice]** Explain gaps (e.g., tax-exempt income) with official proof. After arrival, the business must pay income tax in the province (NB: tax on income earned in NB; NS: "pays the appropriate income tax").
11. **Transfer and use of funds.** Show the bank trail from the source country into Canada and into the business account (NS lists bank statements showing where start-up money came from and the deposit to the business account). BC asks at arrival whether required funds were transferred to a B.C. financial institution.
12. **Settlement vs investment funds.** Keep them separate in every submission. Federal cases (*Shoaie* 2025 FC 12, *Gor* 2026 FC 872) show officers confusing them. SUV legacy files: R98.01(2)(c) needs transferable, unencumbered funds of half LICO, excluding the designated entity's investment.
13. **s.40 risk.** Inflated values, nominee holders, borrowed "show money", undisclosed liabilities or undeclared representatives can lead to provincial refusal (NS 5-year, BC 2-year bars) and an IRCC **IRPA s.40** finding (5-year inadmissibility). Even after SOR/2026-63, IRCC still assesses admissibility, including misrepresentation, on PNP files (R87; IRCC PDI).
14. **Representatives.** Paid representatives must be declared (BC, NB, NS). Flag ghost-consultant risk.

## Consultant pitfalls
- Counting a recent gift in NB (6-month rule).
- Counting loans or property equity toward a minimum personal investment (BC, NS).
- Engaging a verifier before the ITA, or an unlisted verifier.
- Net worth figure in the EOI/registration that the verifier cannot confirm (NS closes the file; BC may refuse for material difference).

## Common refusal reasons
- Unverifiable accumulation; undeclared assets or liabilities; wrong verifier; inconsistent figures between EOI and report.
