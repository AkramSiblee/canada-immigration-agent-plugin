## Must cover
- Start-Up Visa (SUV) genuineness and "primary purpose" refusals (R89, R98.01)
- C11 / R205(a) significant-benefit work permit refusals
- Business plan viability and ability to establish (self-employed, entrepreneur work permits)
- Source of funds and s.40 misrepresentation in business files
- How provincial nominee decisions are challenged; IRCC refusals after nomination; R87(3) history

## Program status (context)
- SUV PR: closed to new files (only 2025-commitment holders who applied by 2026-06-30). SUV work permit: closed to new applicants since 2025-12-19. Self-Employed: paused. See `01-federal-economic/business-and-self-employed.md`. The cases still matter for files in inventory and for C11/PNP business plans.

## Current rules
### Key provisions
- **R89 (artificial transactions):** a self-employed or start-up business class applicant does not meet the requirements if they rest on transactions entered into "primarily for the purpose of acquiring a status or privilege under the Act" rather than for self-employment or the business the commitment was for (H).
- **R98.01(2):** SUV membership needs a valid commitment (under 6 months old), CLB 5, transferable unencumbered settlement funds (half LICO, excluding the designated entity's investment), and a qualifying business (R98.06). Max 5 applicants per business (H).
- **R87 after SOR/2026-63 (in force 2026-03-30):** the province has **"sole responsibility"** to evaluate economic establishment and intent to reside. Old R87(3)-(4) federal **substituted evaluation is gone** (R87(4) repealed). Applies to new files and inventory without an eligibility decision. IRCC still checks identity, a valid unexpired nomination, the R87(5)/(6) passive-investment exclusion, and admissibility (including s.40). Adverse information goes to the province (60-90 days to respond) (H).

### (a) Start-Up Visa: genuineness, commitment, primary purpose
| Case | Outcome | Holding | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Phan*, **2022 FC 916** (2022-06-17) | Dismissed | Officers reasonably found the incubator engagement was primarily to get status (R89(b)). No need to assess R98.06(2) intent first | A commitment certificate does not prove a genuine business. Show real activity |
| *Neri*, **2025 FC 1087** | Dismissed | "Artificial transaction" finding upheld after a holistic look at experience and business progress | Track record and traction matter as much as the certificate |
| *Peyvastegan*, **2025 FC 1599** | Dismissed | Reasonable to find primary purpose was status, even after a chance to add evidence | A PFL response must answer the concern with documents, not assertions |
| *Sharif*, **2026 FC 1039** (2026-08-10) | Dismissed | Three co-founders (Bangladesh). No document showed operations or intent to operate in Canada | Preparatory steps and stated plans are not enough. Show Canadian operations |
| *Majidifard*, **2026 FC 538** (2026-04-23) | Allowed (4 co-founders) | Officer asked for ownership details by PFL, then ignored the reply under a deadline that did not apply | Fairness: an officer cannot request information and then disregard it |
| *Maghami*, **2023 FC 542** | Dismissed | SUV work permit: vague plan, no urgent need to be in Canada | WP requests need a concrete reason the founder must be in Canada now |
| *Karimi*, **2023 FC 411** | Allowed | SUV work permit refused because applicant might not leave if PR failed; unreasonable for a PR-pathway permit | Dual intent is built in; R179 "will not leave" reasoning is weak here |
| *Serimbetov*, **2022 FC 1130** (citation/link unverified 2026-11-20; do not cite yet) | Allowed (unverified) | Same theme as *Karimi*: refusing for not leaving was illogical | As above |
| *Shoaie*, **2025 FC 12** (2025-01-02) | Allowed | SUV work permit: officer said IELTS was missing (it was on file) and treated funds unintelligibly | Separate settlement funds (LICO) from investment funds in the submission |
| *Gor*, **2026 FC 872** | Allowed | SUV work permit: officer ignored sworn statements, a property valuation and the commitment certificate; wrongly discounted a bank statement over a "relationship handler" name | Funds evidence must be engaged with; label every document clearly |

### (b) C11 / R205(a) significant benefit
| Case | Outcome | Holding | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Haghighikafash*, **2025 FC 1601** (2025-09-29) | Allowed | Officer overlooked a Canadian bank statement and gave no reasoning on why a legal-services business lacked significant benefit | Reasons must connect the plan to the "no significant benefit" finding |
| *Misaghi*, **2026 FC 283** (2026-03-02) | Dismissed | Officer could weigh benefit during the permit's validity, not distant projections | Show near-term jobs, spending and Canadian benefit within the permit period |
| *Hosseini*, **2026 FC 298** (2026-03-04) | Dismissed | Applicant bears the burden; officer may expect documentary evidence from Canadian sources of significant benefit | Get Canadian evidence (leases, quotes, LOIs, hiring plans), not just a self-written plan |

### (c) Business plan viability / ability to establish (self-employed)
| Case | Outcome | Holding | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Shiri*, **2026 FC 224** | Allowed | Officer did not explain why the applicant's experience fell short of self-employed requirements | Officers must engage with the experience evidence |
| *Mohammadzadeh*, **2022 FC 75** | Dismissed | Plan lacked detail and feasibility | Detailed, costed plan; show ability and intention |
| *Gur*, **2019 FC 1275** | Dismissed | Claiming abilities and intent is not enough; must show actual ability to create and sustain self-employment | Evidence over statements |
| *Singh*, **2016 FC 904** | Dismissed | Farm plan: no shown knowledge or ability to run the proposed farm | Match the plan to proven skills |
Holdings in (c) are from one secondary summary each (amcaim.ca); official item numbers not confirmed (M/L). Read before citing.

### (d) Source of funds and s.40 in business files
- *Gor* (2026 FC 872) and *Shoaie* (2025 FC 12) above: funds findings set aside where the officer ignored or misread evidence.
- *Ju*, **2026 FC 236**: SINP nominee (work permit and PR) refused for misrepresentation; set aside for breach of procedural fairness. The PR refusal fell with it. Lesson: misrepresentation findings attract a **higher fairness duty** (5-year bar), so the concern must be put clearly. Official item number not confirmed (M).
- Provincial side: provinces refuse for unverifiable net worth or source of funds (BC, NB, NS guides). See `11-forms-fees-processing/source-of-funds-net-worth.md`.
- **No 2024-2026 Federal Court decision squarely upholding an s.40 finding based on business source of funds was found.** Unknown.

### (e) Where to challenge PNP decisions
| Decision | Forum | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Provincial refusal of nomination / application | **Provincial superior court** (judicial review), not Federal Court | Exhaust internal review first where it exists |
| BC PNP refusal | BC PNP **request for review** (PIPA s.7(1)-(2): 30 days from notice if resident in Canada, 60 if not; $500; no new evidence), then **BC Supreme Court** JR by petition (JRPA s.2(1)) | **JR time limit is in the PIPA, not the JRPA:** PIPA s.7(5) allows JR of the director's review decision within **30 days** of the s.7(4)(b) notice if resident in Canada, **60 days** if not (H, BC Laws, current to 2026-11-17). PIPA has no appeal. Cancellation of nomination (PIPA s.6) is **not** internally reviewable (BC EI Base guide, eff. 2024-05-27). **JRPA s.11 is repealed** by *Miscellaneous Statutes Amendment Act, 2026* (Bill 16, s.1; SBC 2026 c.15), in force on Royal Assent (M: from the Bill 16 commencement table and BC Laws repeal note; Royal Assent date not confirmed). Old s.11 was **not** a 60-day limit: it said JR is not barred by passage of time unless an enactment provides otherwise and delay causes substantial prejudice. The JRPA now has no time-limit section (H). The "60 days" figure comes from *Administrative Tribunals Act* s.57, which applies only to tribunals that statute covers; PIPA s.2(2) applies only ATA ss.1, 55 and 61 to the director |
| AB (AAIP) refusal | **Court of King's Bench of Alberta**, originating application for JR, *Alberta Rules of Court* (Alta Reg 124/2010) r.3.15 | r.3.15(2): file **and serve** within **6 months** of the decision; r.13.5 (extension) does not apply (M: secondary summaries only. The official King's Printer PDF was fetched but could not be read as text. OPEN for H) |
| NL (NLPNP) refusal | **Supreme Court of Newfoundland and Labrador** (General Division), originating application, *Rules of the Supreme Court, 1986* Rule 54 (Crown practice: certiorari, mandamus, prohibition) | r.54.02(1) originating application; r.54.06 (certiorari): file and serve "within a reasonable time" after the decision. **No fixed day count.** r.54.01: general rules on extending time apply (H, assembly.nl.ca) |
| PEI (PEI PNP) refusal | Supreme Court of PEI under the *Judicial Review Act*, RSPEI 1988 c J-3 (cited as such in court documents) | **OPEN:** princeedwardisland.ca returned a Radware bot check; the old PDF URL is 404. Time limit not verified |
| NWT (NTNP) refusal | Supreme Court of the Northwest Territories, originating notice under the *Rules of the Supreme Court of the NWT* (R-010-96), judicial review Part | **OPEN:** justice.gov.nt.ca court-rules files now redirect to gnwt.ca; rule text not read. Older NWT case law (secondary) reports r.596: 30 days to file and serve, court may extend. Unverified |
| YT (YNP) refusal | Supreme Court of Yukon, *Rules of Court* Rule 54 "Application for Judicial Review" (title read on yukoncourts.ca PDF) | **OPEN:** PDF text not machine-readable; time limit not verified |
| NB, NS, MB, SK refusals | Court of King's Bench (NB, MB, SK) / Supreme Court of Nova Scotia | NB and NS guides say decisions are final, no appeal; JR is still the route. Rules and time limits not verified here |
| IRCC refusal of PR after nomination, or of a C11 / C60 / T13 work permit | **Federal Court**, with leave (IRPA s.72) | 15 days (decision in Canada) / 60 days (outside) |

### R87(3) substituted evaluation (historical, pre-2026-03-30)
| Case | Outcome | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| *Hassan*, **2019 FC 1096** (PEI) | Allowed (H, read 2026-11-20) | Weight owed to a nomination depends on the federal-provincial agreement wording |
| *Jalil*, **2015 FC 113** (SK) | Upheld | Officer could assess language against the nominated occupation |
| *Kousar*, **2014 FC 12** | Upheld | Officer may focus on the nominated occupation; casual work elsewhere does not show establishment |
Use only for files decided before 2026-03-30 or by analogy. Since SOR/2026-63, an IRCC officer re-assessing establishment or intent to reside is acting **without authority** (IRCC PDI, modified 2026-04-17). That is a strong JR ground for a lawyer to assess.

## Escalation
- **Any judicial review = lawyer referral.** Federal Court (IRPA s.72, leave, 15/60 days) and provincial superior courts (BC Supreme Court, Courts of King's Bench, Supreme Court of Nova Scotia) are outside RCIC scope. See `10-consultant-practice/scope-and-representation.md`.
- RCIC role: preserve deadlines (tell the client the clock is running the day the refusal arrives), file the BC PNP request for review if in scope, order GCMS/ATIP notes, and hand over a clean record.
- Flag s.40 risk and ghost-consultant risk in every business file. Provinces require disclosure of paid representatives (BC, NB, NS).

## Recent changes (2025-2027)
- 2026-03-30: SOR/2026-63 removes federal substituted evaluation for PNP (H)
- 2025-2026: run of SUV refusals upheld on primary purpose/no Canadian operations (*Peyvastegan*, *Neri*, *Sharif*); set-asides on fairness and ignored evidence (*Majidifard*, *Gor*, *Shoaie*) (M)
- 2026: C11 cases stress near-term, documented Canadian benefit (*Misaghi*, *Hosseini*) (M)

## Consultant pitfalls
- Treating a commitment certificate or a provincial support letter as decisive. Neither binds IRCC.
- Mixing settlement funds and investment funds in one figure.
- Answering a PFL with argument instead of documents.
- Advising on R87(3) as if it still applies.
- Missing the 15-day Federal Court window for in-Canada refusals.
- Missing the BC PIPA s.7(5) JR window (30 days in Canada / 60 outside, from the review-decision notice). Alberta r.3.15 is 6 months from the decision with no extension (M).
