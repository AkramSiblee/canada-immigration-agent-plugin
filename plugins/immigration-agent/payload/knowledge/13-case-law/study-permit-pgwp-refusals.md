# Federal Court: study permit, PGWP and student-spouse OWP refusals (2024-2026)

## Must cover
- R216(1)(b) leave-at-end-of-stay, study plan logic, funds, adequacy of reasons/Chinook, PGWP eligibility, spouse OWP linked to students

## How these were verified (read first)
- On 2026-11-17 the official Federal Court site and CanLII returned HTTP 403 to automated reading. Decisions were **not opened**.
- Each citation, date, docket and outcome below was matched across at least two secondary summaries (mainly dadkhah.ca and amcaim.ca case-law posts). Where a summary linked the official decision, the **official item number** is given.
- Rows marked **(CanLII)** have no confirmed official item number. The CanLII URL is built from the neutral citation and was not opened.
- Holdings are **M** across the board. Read the full decision before citing it to a client, an officer or the Court.
- **Federal Court work goes to a licensed lawyer** (see Escalation).

## Current rules

### (a) Leave at end of stay: R216(1)(b), ties, purpose of visit
| Case | Result | Holding | Practice lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Iqbal*, **2026 FC 88** (2026-01-21, IMM-22920-24) | Upheld | Third study permit refusal reasonable on employment, financial and family ties to Pakistan. Onus on applicant; officer need not chase missing information | Repeat refusals: fix the evidence gap, don't resubmit the same file |
| *Golmohammadi*, **2026 FC 428** (2026-04-01, IMM-17687-24) | Set aside | Mother and two minors (Iran): officer called study plans generic and ties weak without weighing the detailed evidence on both | Detailed, specific evidence forces engagement; whole-family travel isn't fatal by itself |
| *Taghizadeh*, **2025 FC 809** (May 2025, Gascon J.) (CanLII) | Set aside | Officer ignored material evidence of family ties in Iran. Often cited as the summary of principles for study permit JRs | Put ties evidence on the record in a form that is hard to overlook |
| *Malekiazar*, **2026 FC 1007** (2026-07-28, IMM-14813-24) | Set aside | Also failed to weigh significant family, professional and financial ties (see (b)) | Same |

### (b) Study plan and program choice
| Case | Result | Holding | Practice lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Malekiazar*, **2026 FC 1007** (2026-07-28) | Set aside | Officer is not a career counsellor. Requiring proof of a promotion or higher salary to show benefit was unreasonable | Benefit can be shown other ways; still give concrete career evidence |
| *Akbar Pour Shandiz*, **2026 FC 255** (2026-02-23, IMM-18758-24) | Set aside | Finding that an MBA made no sense for a lawyer lacked logic; officer ignored a detailed study plan that answered the concern | Explain any apparent career "regression" or switch head-on |
| *Khan*, **2025 FC 276** (2025) | Set aside | All three refusal grounds unjustified | Multi-ground refusals can still fall if each ground is unreasoned |
| *Aghajari*, **2026 FC 661** (2026-05-22, IMM-13690-24) | Upheld | Vague MBA study plan; no evidence on how the conditions in a conditional acceptance letter would be met | Answer every LOA condition with evidence |
| *Azadi*, **2026 FC 760** (2026-06-09, IMM-17270-24) | Upheld | No justification to switch from virtual to in-person study; sudden large deposit unexplained (see (c)) | Explain why the program must be done in Canada, in person |
| *Mohammod*, **2025 FC 1512** (2025-09-12, IMM-11079-24) | Upheld | Costly 3-month English course available at home; officer may weigh cost and local availability in a holistic assessment with weak ties | "Cheaper at home" is a valid factor, not decisive alone. Address it in the plan |
| *Ghasemi*, **2025 FC 1072** (2025) | Upheld | Unclear benefit of the program given its expense | Cost-benefit must be explained by the applicant |
| *Rezvani Gilkolaei*, **2025 FC 194** (2025) | Upheld | Generic study plan, vague employer letters: officer "entitled to respond to boilerplate with boilerplate" | Template study plans invite template refusals |

### (e) PGWP
| Case | Result | Holding | Practice lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Saqeb*, **2025 FC 845** (2025-05-08) (CanLII) | Upheld | Mostly part-time studies on counsellors' advice for mental health: officers have **no discretion** to waive full-time requirement | Medical reasons don't create an exception. Advise students before they drop to part-time |
| *Singh*, **2026 FC 437** (2026-04-02, IMM-676-25) | Upheld | Same point: full-time is mandatory even with medical evidence. Distinguishes *Sugagata*, *Munyanyi*, *Tcerkovnaia* | Same |
| *Singh*, **2026 FC 422** (2026-03-31, IMM-24085-24) | Upheld | Part-time study is not "authorized leave"; a DLI's permission to go part-time doesn't override IRCC rules | Check authorized-leave rules, not school policy |
| *Singh*, **2025 FC 1935** (2025-12-08, IMM-15147-24) | Upheld | Part-time allowance covers only the final session; retaking a failed course is not an extenuating circumstance | Failed courses can break eligibility. Plan course loads |
| *Kaur*, **2025 FC 1478** (2025-09-08, IMM-18826-24) | Upheld | Mandatory work placement, not the earlier part-time term, was the final session | Co-op/placement after a part-time term defeats the final-session exception |
| *Chahal*, **2025 FC 1910** (2025-12-01, IMM-1417-25) | Upheld | Course withdrawal in Summer 2020 broke full-time status; a minor officer error doesn't matter where the refusal facts are undisputed | JR is not a hunt for small errors |
| *Dhamu*, **2026 FC 562** (2026-04-29, IMM-1702-25) | Upheld | Applicant didn't provide the requested credential assessment or explain why | Always answer IRCC document requests or explain inability in writing |
| *Sugagata*, **2024 FC 1436** (2024, Grammond J.) (CanLII) | Set aside | Officer ignored medical and DLI evidence explaining one part-time term; no sign of "best judgment" | **Conflict:** later cases (*Saqeb*, *Singh* 2026 FC 437) say no discretion. Treat *Sugagata* as limited to failure to engage with evidence |
| *Kaur*, **2026 FC 173** (2026-02-06, IMM-17577-24) | Upheld | Restoration covers only the status last held (worker can't restore to student); outside 90 days anyway | Track which status the client last held and the 90-day clock |
| *Jasmeet Kaur*, **2026 FC 1095** (2026-08-25, IMM-5153-25) | Upheld | TRP after PGWP ineligibility needs a compelling reason; options like a work permit from abroad weigh against | TRP is a high bar, not a PGWP substitute |
| *Ocran*, **2025 FC 517** (2025-03-20, IMM-7827-24) (CanLII) | Set aside | TRP refusal ignored evidence and the clash between the 6-month unauthorized-work bar and the PGWP application window | Raise timing conflicts expressly in TRP submissions |

### Themes
- Standard of review: reasonableness (*Vavilov*). Fairness issues (veiled credibility) are reviewed for fairness.
- **Onus on the applicant**; no duty to request missing evidence (*Iqbal*, *Kaji*).
- **Engagement wins cases:** detailed study plans, ties and funds evidence that the officer ignored (*Akbar Pour Shandiz*, *Golmohammadi*, *Alademomi*).
- **Generic in, generic out** (*Rezvani Gilkolaei*, *Aghajari*).
- **PGWP is near-strict liability on full-time status** (2025-2026 line). JR rarely helps unless the officer ignored evidence of actual compliance.
- A win sends the file to a new officer. It does not issue the permit (see *Rafique*, 2026 FC 658, a work permit case).

## Escalation
- **JR = refer to a licensed lawyer.** RCICs can't appear in Federal Court.
- Leave required. Deadline to file the application for leave and JR: **15 days** (decision made in Canada, e.g. PGWP, restoration, in-Canada study permit extension) or **60 days** (decision made outside Canada, e.g. study permit from abroad), from notification (IRPA s.72(2)(b)). (H)
- Order GCMS notes immediately; refer early so counsel has time.

## How to use (reapply vs JR)
- **Reapply** when the refusal reflects a gap in your evidence (unexplained deposits, generic plan, missing enrolment proof, unanswered document request, LOA conditions). It's faster and the court can't consider new evidence anyway.
- **Consider JR (via lawyer)** when the record already answered the officer's concern and the reasons ignore it, misread it (GIC, joint funds), make an unstated credibility finding, or give no reasoning at all.
- **PGWP:** if part-time outside the final session or outside the 180-day window, JR is weak. Look at other options (new study, other work permit, TRP with a compelling reason) and check `knowledge/14-policy-tracker/`.
- Don't reapply and JR the same decision without the lawyer's advice; a fresh decision can moot the JR.

## Recent changes (2025-2027)
- 2025-2026: consistent line upholding PGWP refusals for part-time terms (*Saqeb*, *Kaur*, *Chahal*, *Singh* x3) (M)
- 2026: repeated set-asides where officers ignored study plans or misread funds (*Bista*, *Akbar Pour Shandiz*, *Alademomi*, *Golmohammadi*, *Malekiazar*) (M)

## Consultant pitfalls
- Don't promise an outcome from a case. Each turns on its record.
- Flag misrepresentation risk (IRPA s.40) on funds documents and transcripts.
- Watch for ghost-consultant prepared study plans (templates fail, per *Rezvani Gilkolaei*).
