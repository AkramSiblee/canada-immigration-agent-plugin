# Misrepresentation (IRPA s.40)

## Must cover
- 5-year bar, innocent mistake, responses, appeals

## Current rules
- **s.40(1)(a):** directly or indirectly misrepresenting or withholding **material facts** on a relevant matter that induces or could induce an error.
- **s.40(1)(b):** being sponsored by a person found inadmissible for misrepresentation (only if the Minister is satisfied, s.40(2)(b)).
- **s.40(1)(c):** a refugee/protection decision vacated.
- **s.40(1)(d):** ceasing to be a citizen for fraud (Citizenship Act s.10).
- **Bar: 5 years** (s.40(2)(a)) from a final determination outside Canada, or from enforcement of the removal order if in Canada. **No PR application during the bar** (s.40(3)).
- In Canada: s.44 report, then an **exclusion order** (5-yr ARC requirement, IRPR s.225(3)).
- **AMP (SOR/2026-220, new IRPR Part 19.2, ss.315.45-315.6):** targets **paid representatives/advisers**, not applicants (see the AMP section below). The applicant's own exposure is still s.40 (refusal, 5-yr bar).
- **Appeals:** IAD only for sponsored spouse/partner/child (s.64(3)), and PR removal orders. Otherwise JR (lawyer).
- **Innocent mistake:** a narrow case-law exception where the person honestly and reasonably believed they weren't withholding material information, and the knowledge was beyond their control (Federal Court jurisprudence; M: see 13-case-law).
- Applicants are responsible for representatives' and agents' misstatements.
- **Voluntary correction:** *Ganeshalingam*, 2024 FC 1437 (granted): decision-maker must assess a voluntary correction against the statutory question of whether it could induce an error; contrast the line that catching a misrepresentation before assessment doesn't help on materiality (*Oloumi*, 2012 FC 428, as cited there). *Lam*, 2025 FC 102 (dismissed): innocent mistake is narrow, truly extraordinary circumstances; applicant bears responsibility even for others' errors and unintentional ones. Both read 2026-11-09 from the Federal Court site.
- **Correction route:** IRCC web form, "Update your application" (page 2026-06-01). No IRCC page found on correcting a client's own error in a submitted e-APR.
- **Procedural fairness:** a PFL normally comes first (officers must disclose concerns and allow a response; case law) (M).

## Administrative monetary penalties for representatives (SOR/2026-220)
Checked 2026-11-17 and **re-checked 2026-11-20: still no dedicated IRCC AMP guideline or misrepresentation PDI found** (IRCC committed to publish new misrepresentation PDIs by September 2026; not seen on canada.ca). The AMP process page exists: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/partners-service-providers/break-rules-advice-representation.html (notice of violation; 30 days to pay or request review). IRCC's 2026 management action plan committed to publishing new misrepresentation PDIs "by September 2026"; none located on canada.ca as of 2026-11-17. The RIAS says officer guidance, public guidance and notice templates exist; the public guidance is the IRCC "break the rules" page below. (H for what is published; unknown for internal officer guidance.)
- **In force:** SOR/2026-220 is in force on registration, **2026-10-23** (s.2; Gazette II published 2026-11-04). IRCC news release of **2026-10-28** says "starting today". **Conflict: 23 vs 28 Oct.** Use 23 Oct (the regulation text) and flag it. Not retroactive: no penalty for conduct before coming into force (IRCC page) (H).
- **Who:** a person who, for consideration, represents or advises (or offers to) in connection with an EOI or an IRPA application/proceeding: licensed RCICs, lawyers, other authorized reps **and** unauthorized paid advisers, including outside Canada (s.315.46). Entities for which the person conducts business are covered for document requests (s.315.49). **Applicants are not penalized parties** (H).
- **Violations and baselines:** unauthorized paid representation **$5,000** (s.315.47); knowingly misrepresenting, or counselling/helping a person to misrepresent **$15,000** each (s.315.48(a)-(c)); failing to produce documents on inspection **$10,000** × multiplier (s.315.49(3), 315.55) (H).
- **Formula (s.315.54):** (A + B + C) × D. A = baseline; B = **$15,000** impact amount if the s.315.48 violation caused an error in administering the Act; C = financial advantage (all amounts received in connection with the violation); D = prior-violation multiplier **0.5** (none), **1.0** (one, or one notice/same-day notices), **1.5** (otherwise). No discretionary aggravating/mitigating factors beyond this. Cap **$1.5M per notice of violation** (s.315.56; also combined with a same-day Citizenship Regulations NOV) (H).
- **Defences/limits:** document-production failure justified by all reasonable efforts or good faith (s.315.49(6)); solicitor-client privilege / professional secrecy: may file an affidavit instead of producing documents (ss.315.49(4)-(5), 315.51(1)(b)) (H).
- **Process:** officer inspection on reasonable grounds → **preliminary notice** → **30 days** for written submissions, privilege affidavit or extension request (extension for reasonable justification, s.315.51(3)) → **notice of violation** (balance of probabilities, reasons, s.315.52). Notices deemed received 30 days after sending. Once an NOV issues, no new penalties for earlier conduct (s.315.53) (H).
- **Review:** instead of paying, written request within **30 days** of NOV receipt, stating grounds; **no new evidence**. GIC-appointed reviewer may cancel, confirm or correct. Then Federal Court JR with leave (RIAS; refer to a lawyer). No request = deemed to have committed the violation (s.315.58(3)) (H). Review link: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/partners-service-providers/break-rules-advice-representation.html#review
- **Payment:** within **30 days** of NOV (or of the reviewer's decision), or a payment agreement with the Minister; IRCC Finance 1-888-448-4426. **Paying or entering an agreement = admission** of the violation (s.315.57(2)) (H).
- **Non-payment:** the regulations set no specific consequence; RIAS refers to follow-up and "additional collection activities". Publication proceeds regardless (unknown beyond that).
- **Publication (s.315.6):** name, business/workplace and address, date, violation, amount, paid or not; not before the 30-day review window ends; removed if overturned on JR (RIAS). IRCC online registry expected **2027** (news release) (H).
- **Relationship to s.40:** separate regimes. AMPs target the representative; the applicant can still be refused and barred under s.40 (applicants are responsible for reps' misstatements). AMPs run alongside CICC/law society discipline and CBSA/RCMP criminal enforcement (IRPA s.91, s.126-127) (IRCC page) (H).

## Recent changes (2025-2027)
- 2026-10-23: AMPs for paid representatives (misrepresentation, unauthorized practice, document refusal) (SOR/2026-220; IRCC release dated 2026-10-28) (H)

## Consultant pitfalls
- **Always flag s.40 risk** (CLAUDE.md rule 5): prior refusals not disclosed, family members omitted, fake job offers or bank letters, inflated experience letters.
- **Ghost consultants:** clients with prior applications by unlicensed agents. Get GCMS notes to see what was submitted.
- PFL response: admit and explain where appropriate. The test is materiality and the reasonableness of the belief. Evidence of genuine error helps.
- Consultants who knowingly submit false information, or counsel/help a client to, face CICC discipline **and** AMPs ($15,000 base, +$15,000 if approved on it, + fees received, × multiplier). Keep verification records. Don't pay an NOV without advice: payment is an admission. Assert privilege by affidavit, not by silence.

## Common refusal reasons
- Undisclosed refusals or criminality; fraudulent documents; undeclared relationships or children; false employment

## Re-verify before advising
- Misrepresentation PDI (promised by Sept 2026; not found 2026-11-17); first published AMPs / registry; in-force date conflict (23 vs 28 Oct 2026)
