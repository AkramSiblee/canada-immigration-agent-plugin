# Misrepresentation (IRPA s.40)

## Must cover
- 5-year bar, innocent mistake, responses, appeals

## Current rules
- **s.40(1)(a):** directly or indirectly misrepresenting or withholding **material facts** on a relevant matter that induces or could induce an error.
- **s.40(1)(b):** being sponsored by a person found inadmissible for misrepresentation (only if the Minister is satisfied, s.40(2)(b)).
- **s.40(1)(c):** a refugee/protection decision vacated.
- **s.40(1)(d):** ceasing to be a citizen for fraud (Citizenship Act s.10).
- **Bar: 5 years** (s.40(2)(a)) from a final determination outside Canada, or from enforcement of the removal order if in Canada. **No PR application during the bar** (s.40(3)).
- In Canada: s.44 report, then an **exclusion order** (5-yr ARC requirement, IRPR s.225(3)).
- **AMP (SOR/2026-220, from 2026-10-23):** baseline **$15,000**, plus $15,000 if it induces an error; names published. Applies in addition to the bar.
- **Appeals:** IAD only for sponsored spouse/partner/child (s.64(3)), and PR removal orders. Otherwise JR (lawyer).
- **Innocent mistake:** a narrow case-law exception where the person honestly and reasonably believed they weren't withholding material information, and the knowledge was beyond their control (Federal Court jurisprudence; M: see 13-case-law).
- Applicants are responsible for representatives' and agents' misstatements.
- **Voluntary correction:** *Ganeshalingam*, 2024 FC 1437 (granted): decision-maker must assess a voluntary correction against the statutory question of whether it could induce an error; contrast the line that catching a misrepresentation before assessment doesn't help on materiality (*Oloumi*, 2012 FC 428, as cited there). *Lam*, 2025 FC 102 (dismissed): innocent mistake is narrow, truly extraordinary circumstances; applicant bears responsibility even for others' errors and unintentional ones. Both read 2026-11-09 from the Federal Court site.
- **Correction route:** IRCC web form, "Update your application" (page 2026-06-01). No IRCC page found on correcting a client's own error in a submitted e-APR.
- **Procedural fairness:** a PFL normally comes first (officers must disclose concerns and allow a response; case law) (M).

## Recent changes (2025-2027)
- 2026-10-23: monetary penalties for misrepresentation (SOR/2026-220) (H)

## Consultant pitfalls
- **Always flag s.40 risk** (CLAUDE.md rule 5): prior refusals not disclosed, family members omitted, fake job offers or bank letters, inflated experience letters.
- **Ghost consultants:** clients with prior applications by unlicensed agents. Get GCMS notes to see what was submitted.
- PFL response: admit and explain where appropriate. The test is materiality and the reasonableness of the belief. Evidence of genuine error helps.
- Consultants who knowingly submit false information face CICC discipline **and** AMPs. Keep verification records.

## Common refusal reasons
- Undisclosed refusals or criminality; fraudulent documents; undeclared relationships or children; false employment
