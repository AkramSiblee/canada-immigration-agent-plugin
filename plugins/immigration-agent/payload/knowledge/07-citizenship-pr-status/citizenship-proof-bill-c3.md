# Proof of citizenship and Bill C-3

## Must cover
- First-generation limit removal, 1,095-day rule, Lost Canadians, processing

## Current rules
**Bill C-3 (S.C. 2025, c.5) in force 2025-12-15.**

### Born abroad **before** 2025-12-15
- Likely a citizen if a parent was a Canadian citizen at the birth, **at any generation** (the first-generation limit is removed retroactively). s.3(1.5) covers parents who died before coming into force.

### Born abroad **on or after** 2025-12-15
- Citizen by descent only if the Canadian parent (born or naturalized abroad) was **physically present in Canada for at least 1,095 days before the birth** (s.3(3)).
- Stateless children (s.5(5)): grant if under 23, the parent was a citizen at birth, and 1,095 days in Canada in the 4 yrs before applying.

### Adopted abroad
- Adopted **before** 2025-12-15: s.5.1 direct grant (also second generation or later, if the parent had 1,095 days before the adoption, per IRCC).
- Adopted **on or after**: s.5.1(4) bar unless the adoptive parent meets the 1,095-day test.
- s.5.1 grants are refused where the adoption was mainly for status or created no genuine parent-child relationship.

### Proof
- **Apply for a citizenship certificate** to confirm ("you must apply for a citizenship certificate to find out for sure"). Fee **$75**. Add 3-4 months if applying through a mission outside Canada/US. IRCC estimate for a new proof application: **about 34 months** (about 130,700 waiting; IRCC forward-looking data 2026-10-09).
- Inventory: 135,965 proof applications not finalized; 38,355 issued since 2026-01-01 (data 2026-08-31).
- Renunciation is free for those who became citizens automatically under the 2009, 2015 or 2025 changes.
- Form: **CIT 0001 (08-2026)**. Section 12 asks applicants born on or after 2025-12-15 whether the citizen parent had 1,095 days in Canada before the birth; if Yes, attach **CIT 0555** (parent's physical presence calculation) and proof. For a person adopted outside Canada, CIT 0001 is only for a replacement certificate (H, form read 2026-11-10).

## Recent changes (2025-2027)
- 2025-12-15: Bill C-3 in force. First-generation limit removed; 1,095-day test for future births abroad (H) - confirmed

## Consultant pitfalls
- **Multigenerational or complex C-3 cases: refer to a lawyer** (CLAUDE.md rule 4), especially deceased parents, pre-1947 ancestry, or adoption chains.
- A newly recognized citizen **can't be sponsored or hold PR**, and shouldn't apply for visas as a foreigner. Get the certificate first.
- Citizenship may bring obligations (taxes, military in other countries) and dual nationality issues. Flag them.
- For births after 2025-12-15, document the parent's 1,095 days (travel history, tax, school records) **before** the birth registration.

## Common refusal reasons
- Parent's citizenship at birth not proven; 1,095 days not proven (post-2025 births); adoption not genuine

## Re-verify before advising
- IRCC "Lost Canadians" page; C-3 processing times; any regulations on the 1,095-day proof
